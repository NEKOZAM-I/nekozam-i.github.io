Why I'm rebuilding Pageify's homepage

The old layout worked, but it never felt like mine.

Jul 29, 2026 · 5 min read

Pageify's homepage has technically "worked" since the day I shipped it. People landed on it, understood what the tool did, and moved on. Nothing was broken. And that was exactly the problem — it was fine, and fine isn't why I build things.

Where it started

The first version was a page I threw together in an evening to have something to point people to. It borrowed layout decisions from a dozen other builder-tool landing pages, because I hadn't figured out yet what Pageify was supposed to feel like. It looked competent and said nothing.

What changed

The more people I watched actually use Pageify, the clearer it got that the product has a personality — quick, a little irreverent, built by someone who got annoyed at how clunky every other "no-code" builder felt. The homepage never reflected any of that. It was a brochure for a tool that doesn't act like a brochure.

So the rebuild isn't about new features or a trendier layout. It's about closing the gap between how Pageify feels to use and how it looks before you've touched it.

What's next

Bento-style sections for the feature breakdown, tighter copy, and a lot less filler. If it doesn't earn its spot on the page, it's getting cut. I'll post the before/after once it's live.
